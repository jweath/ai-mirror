# Build Journal: AI Mirror

Starting with Sprint 3 on **October 15**, your Build Journal becomes a record of what you tried, what you observed, what changed your thinking, and what you want to learn next. This is individual work, including for the two students sharing a project. No earlier journals are owed.

Your assistant can collect evidence and suggest possibilities. **You write the reflection.** That is the gym work. Preserve and link the AI starting point so we can see what you received and what you made of it. A link is transparency, not an authorship detector.

## Get started

1. Download [the single AI Mirror file](../skills/ai-mirror/SKILL.md). Attach it to your existing assistant or paste its contents, then say: “Use AI Mirror to help me start Sprint 3.” A coding assistant with your repository can save files; a chat-only assistant can give you copy/save steps.
2. Write your individual goals and smallest meaningful test. Consider entrepreneurial learning and an appropriate AI/technical learning goal. What would make you persist, pivot, or stop? What will you personally verify?
3. After each Tuesday/Thursday class, write a short response in your project channel: what changed, the evidence, and what is still muddy. Other-day updates are optional. Each partner responds individually.

Suggested effort: kickoff 10–15 minutes, class exit ticket 2–3 minutes, midpoint 5–8 minutes, wrap-up 15–20 minutes, peer exchange 5–8 minutes. These are guides, not timers or word-count tests.

## At the sprint midpoint and end

At midpoint, revisit your initial goal and record changes with a date and reason. At wrap-up, use Hypothesis → Build → Measure → Learn to explain your evidence and decisions. Include something you personally verified about AI/technical work, a short answer to your assistant's learning question, what remains muddy, feedback you used or questioned, and your next goal. “I'm still unsure” is a valid answer; the assistant gives no score.

Keep your original kickoff. Save AI evidence briefs separately. Link to exact versions rather than a moving `main` branch. Your entry can be concise; 300–500 words will often suffice. Faithful dictation is welcome. AI may identify an unclear sentence, but you revise it. It must not write or polish your reflective prose for you.

## Studio feedback

Post your human-written journal text and exact-version link in your project channel so classmates can read it even if your code repository is private. The assigned peer replies in that thread with one specific strength tied to your goal, one evidence question, and one small next test. Write your own feedback. The author replies with what they will use or question, then captures that decision in their journal.

Customer secrets, personal concerns, and sensitive team issues belong in a private note to John. Use `/mirror muddy …` if connected, or the usual private channel. You can mention “private follow-up with instructor” in the shared entry without revealing it. No one needs to disclose personal material to earn credit. John will bring useful, non-identifying themes back to class and invite you to revisit them.

## Submission

Save `journal/<your-id>/<sprint>/reflection.md` in your project repository. Make one GitHub issue titled `Learning journal: <your name> — <sprint>` linking:

- The human journal at a full commit SHA.
- The dated AI starting brief(s) at stable links.
- Your Slack peer exchange, when available, and any feedback still pending.

Verify the links and close the issue as your submission receipt. To revise, add a new commit and comment on the same issue with the new link. With the service connected, `/mirror submit JOURNAL_COMMIT_URL ISSUE_URL` records the receipt for John. The bot is optional; the GitHub issue is sufficient. eLC only records grades.

## What earns credit

Use the existing **10-point Build Journal allocation**, with the two remaining sprint wrap-ups worth 5 points each. Kickoffs, midpoint notes, exit tickets, and peer exchanges support the wrap-up; they do not create additional attendance points. If an accommodation requires another submission format, arrange it with John while keeping the same evidence and authorship expectations.

| In each wrap-up | Points | What the instructor looks for |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Specific evidence and AI transparency | 1.5 | Inspectable examples, linked starting material, clear distinction between planned work and observed results |
| Reasoning and learning | 1.5 | Your interpretation, a decision you reconsidered or justified, honest limits |
| AI/technical judgment | 1 | What you personally checked or can explain, matched to your learning goal |
| Feedback and next step | 1 | Thoughtful use/questioning of feedback and a concrete next test or goal |

Honest failed experiments can earn full credit. We do not grade feature volume, commit counts, polished prose, confidence, or the AI's opinion of you. Missing peer feedback is not automatically your failure: document your request and use available feedback. The instructor assigns grades; the system does not.

Your journals also supply evidence for the 3.5-minute progress presentations and individual Field Guide: annotated prompts/workflows, judgments about tools, and five entrepreneurial lessons written by you.
